Defaults of a new boiler room project.

From photos to a ranked plan.

A project walks six steps, one page each. Every step hands the next one something it can measure.

  1. Capture

    Upload 360° panoramas of your floor. World Labs Marble turns them into a walkable 3D world.

    world.spz · collider.glb · pano.png

  2. Twin

    List your machines and draw how material flows between them. Gemini drafts the flow from your description and finds the machines in your photos.

    intake → staging rack → boiler-1 → header → boiler-2 → inspection hold → boiler-3 → dispatch

  3. Scenario

    Set the conditions to test against, in plain words: a heatwave, a rush order, half the maintenance team off sick.

    34 °C · demand 340 · target 300 batches

  4. Agents

    Put a crew on the floor: a planner, technicians and a supervisor, each with a personality.

    Priya, aggressive · Marek, by the book · Sam, cautious

  5. Results

    Run the shift and watch the agents decide in the 3D world. Compare them with standard rules over many simulated days, then pick changes, run them on a copy of your twin, and apply the ones that work.

    10 days by default · up to 60

  6. New plant

    See the applied changes ranked by how much they matter, with Nano Banana drawing them into photos of your plant.

    gains +19 · +8 · +5 · +3 batches · sample run

One shift, read out.

The crew works the shift once in the twin. Its playbook and standard rules, the fixed way of running the floor with no agents, then work the same shift on each seeded day. Every reading below comes from one sample run of the boiler room.

Ten seeds, the same ten days for every change.

The simulation is deterministic for a given seed. Day one replays the run’s seed and each further day takes the next, so standard rules, the crew and every change meet exactly the same days. One lucky day cannot pass for the plant’s real performance.
